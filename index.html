Invisalign uses clear, removable aligners to straighten teeth without metal brackets or wires. Dental Implants

Dental implants replace a missing tooth with a post placed in the jaw and a crown that looks and works like a natural tooth. They are built to last, they keep nearby teeth from shifting, and they let people chew and speak with confidence. The office handles implant care from the first consult through placement and the final crown, and Dr. Weiss has years of hands-on work in this area. A broken implant crown or a loose implant is one more reason people call for urgent help, and the team will check the implant, the gum around it, and the bite before deciding on a fix.

Emergency

Urgent visits cover severe tooth pain, broken or chipped teeth, knocked-out teeth, lost crowns or fillings, swelling, and bleeding that will not stop. When you call, the team asks where it hurts, what happened, and whether you have swelling or a fever, then finds the earliest slot that day. Treatment depends on the cause: a filling or temporary crown for a break, antibiotics and drainage for an infection, or a plan for a root canal when the nerve is involved. The office keeps room in the day for these calls because tooth pain does not follow a schedule, and neither should the help.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast can I be seen for a tooth emergency?

Same-day slots are set aside for urgent calls, and the office aims to see people the day they call when the schedule allows. Call (561) 260-5575 as early in the day as you can, describe the pain and what happened, and the team will give you the earliest time. If you call late in the day, they will still try to fit you in or advise you on safe steps until the next morning.

What counts as a dental emergency?

Severe pain, a broken or knocked-out tooth, a lost crown or filling with sharp pain, swelling in the face or gum, and bleeding that will not stop all count. A dull ache that comes and goes may still need a prompt visit, but it is less urgent than swelling with a fever, which can signal infection. When in doubt, call and describe it; the team can tell you whether to come in today.

What should I do if a tooth gets knocked out?

Hold the tooth by the crown, not the root, rinse it gently without scrubbing, and try to place it back in the socket if you can do so without force. If that is not possible, keep it in milk or in your cheek and get to the office within the hour. Time matters more than anything else here, so call on the way and let the team know you are coming.

Will an urgent visit cost a lot?

The first visit usually covers an exam and X-rays, and the dentist explains the options and their costs before starting work. Prices depend on what the tooth needs, from a simple filling to a crown or root canal. The office works with a range of insurance plans and offers financing through CareCredit and Cherry, so ask for the full picture before you decide.

Do you take my insurance?

The office works with a variety of insurance providers to help make care more affordable. It does not accept Affinity, Fidelis, Health First, or Medicaid, so check your plan before you book. Bring your insurance card to the visit, and the team can check your benefits and explain what your plan covers for urgent care.

What should I bring to the visit?

Bring a photo ID, your insurance card, a list of medicines and allergies, and any broken pieces of tooth, crown, or filling in a clean container. Note when the pain started, what makes it worse, and whether you have had a fever or swelling. Those details help the dentist find the cause faster and choose the right treatment the first time.

What happens after the urgent visit?

You leave with pain relief, written aftercare, and a follow-up visit already booked. Some teeth need a second visit for a crown or root canal, and the team will set that up before you go.
